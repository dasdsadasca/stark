                                                                                                                                                                   The `StarknetTokenBridge.sol` contract allows users to deposit ERC20 tokens from Layer 1 to Layer 2 (Starknet) and withdraw them back to Layer 1. However, when a user deposits tokens from Layer 1 to Layer 2 using the `deposit` function, the fee parameter is not validated against the actual transaction value. This could lead to a situation where a user specifies a `fee` value that is greater than the actual `msg.value`, resulting in the contract potentially sending a message with an incorrect fee amount. This could lead to unexpected behavior in the L2 contract and potentially disrupt the bridging process or cause loss of funds.                                                                                                                           I. (In these times when it is generally known, that all places are filled with complaints concerning the difficulty of travelling, eſpecially for those who are English-men, whether it be that they are ſuſpected by the Natives of being Spies, or upon what other account I know not; it will not be perhaps unacceptable to many, to ſee the true account of my late Travels through the Dominions of the King of <hi>Spain,</hi> where I found the People infinitely civil to me, as being a Subject of the King of <hi>Great Britain</hi>; and I cannot ſufficiently admire the Courteſie of the <hi>Spaniards,</hi> who ſhewed me all the favour imaginable; for when I was at <hi>Naples,</hi> the Vice-Roy thereof gave me his own Paſsport, and a Letter of Recommendation to the Governour of <p>
                     <hi>Ca<g ref="char:EOLhyphen"/>tania,</hi> to ſhew me what was rare and worthy the obſervation of a Stranger in <hi>Sicily,</hi> and alſo gave me leave to Travel into <hi>Malta</hi> with the ſame freedom, as if I had been one of his Family; ſo that I have been oblig'd to ſay that his Catholick Majeſty has not ſo much as one diſaffected Perſon in all his Domini<g ref="char:EOLhyphen"/>ons, the <hi>Napolitans</hi> not daring to ſhew their hatred openly to the Government, for fear of the <hi>Spaniards</hi> that are amongſt them. For the better un<g ref="char:EOLhyphen"/>derſtanding of this Voyage, I ſhall divide it into two parts, the firſt will give you an account of the Curioſities of the places which I ſaw in the Iſland of <hi>Sicily,</hi> and the ſecond will inform you of what I obſerved at <hi>Malta.</hi>
                  </p>
                  <p>And firſt for the Kingdom of <hi>Sicily,</hi> the ancient <hi>Trinacria,</hi> once the Granary of <hi>Rome,</hi> and the Seat of the <hi>Roman</hi> Empire in the Eaſt, and now the Granary of all <hi>Italy,</hi> and the moſt conſiderable Iſland in the <hi>Mediterranean</hi> Sea; I ſhall confine my ſelf to give you an account of its three principal Cities, <hi>Meſſina, Palermo,</hi> and <hi>Catania.</hi>
                  </p>
                  <p>
                     <hi>Meſſina</hi> is ſituated upon the narrow Streight, call'd <hi>Il Faro di Meſſina,</hi> which ſeparates <hi>Sicily</hi> from <hi>Calabria;</hi> it is a large and populous City, and the ſecond in the Kingdom, though the Vice-Roy uſually reſides at <hi>Palermo.</hi> This City has a very noble and ſpacious Harbour, capable of receiving the greateſt Fleets, and is defended by a ſtrong Cittadel, built upon a Rock at the entrance of the Port. The Town is large and well built, the Streets broad, the Churches and Palaces ſtately and magnificent: amongſt which the Dome or Cathedral is a vaſt Pile of Building, but of the <hi>Gothick</hi> order; the Roof is ſupported by above 100 Pillars of Marble, and the Walls are covered with Moſaick Work. The Senate-Houſe, which is a modern Building, is very beautiful, and the <hi>Piazza</hi> before it is adorned with a noble Fountain. The Vice-Roy's Palace, where the Governour reſides, is a large and ſtately Edifice, and the Arſenal is well furniſhed with Arms for 20000 Men. There are many other publick Buildings in this City, which are very remarkable, as the Hoſpital for the Poor, the Colledge of the Jeſuits, and ſeveral Monaſteries and Nunneries. The Inhabitants are generally civil and courteous to Strangers, but very jealous of their Wives, whom they keep under great reſtraint. They are much addicted to Pleaſure and Luxury, and are great admirers of Muſick and Poetry. This City has often ſuffered by Earthquakes, particularly in the Years 1693, and 1783, when it was almoſt totally deſtroyed. It has been ſubject to ſeveral Revolutions, and was taken by the <hi>Normans</hi> in the Year 1060, by the <hi>French</hi> in 1282, and by the <hi>Spaniards</hi> in 1504, under whom it now remains.</p>
                  <p>
                     <hi>Palermo</hi> is the Capital City of <hi>Sicily,</hi> and the Seat of the Vice-Roy. It is ſituated on the North ſide of the Iſland, in a large and fruitful Plain, environed with Mountains. It is a very ancient City, and is ſaid to have been built by the <hi>Phoenicians.</hi> It is large and populous, and contains about 120000 Inhabitants. The Streets are generally narrow and irregular, but there are ſome that are broad and ſtraight, particularly the <hi>Caſſaro,</hi> which runs through the middle of the City from the Sea to the Royal Palace. The Houſes are for the moſt part well built, and many of them are adorned with Marble Pillars and Balconies. The principal publick Buildings are the Royal Palace, the Cathedral, the Senate-Houſe, the Univerſity, and ſeveral Churches and Monaſteries. The Royal Palace is a vaſt and magnificent Structure, ſituated on an Eminence, and commands a fine Proſpect of the City and the Sea. It was formerly the Reſidence of the <hi>Norman</hi> Kings, and is now inhabited by the Vice-Roy. The Cathedral is a large <hi>Gothick</hi> Building, but has nothing very remarkable in it. The Senate-Houſe is a noble Edifice, adorned with Statues and Paintings. The Univerſity is famous for the Study of Law and Phyſick. There are many beautiful Churches in <hi>Palermo,</hi> particularly that of St. <hi>Catharine,</hi> which is richly adorned with Marble, Jaſper, and Agate. The Harbour is large and commodious, but not ſo ſecure as that of <hi>Meſſina.</hi> The Inhabitants of <hi>Palermo</hi> are very polite and civil, and are much addicted to Pleaſures and Diverſions. They are great Lovers of Muſick, and have an excellent Opera here. The Ladies are generally handſome, but very reſerved, and ſeldom appear abroad without Veils.</p>
                  <p>
                     <hi>Catania</hi> is an ancient and famous City, ſituated on the Eaſtern Coaſt of <hi>Sicily,</hi> at the foot of Mount <hi>Aetna.</hi> It was founded by a Colony of <hi>Chalcidians</hi> from <hi>Naxos,</hi> about 730 Years before CHRIST. It has been often ruined by Earthquakes, and the Eruptions of <hi>Aetna,</hi> particularly in the Year 1693, when it was almoſt totally deſtroyed. It has been ſince rebuilt in a more regular and beautiful manner, and is now one of the handſomeſt Cities in <hi>Sicily.</hi> The Streets are broad and ſtraight, and the Houſes are built of black Lava, which gives them a ſingular and melancholy Aſpect. The principal Buildings are the Cathedral, the Univerſity, the Senate-Houſe, and ſeveral Churches and Convents. The Cathedral is a noble Structure, dedicated to St. <hi>Agatha,</hi> the Patroneſs of the City. The Univerſity is one of the moſt ancient in <hi>Europe,</hi> being founded in the Year 1445. The Inhabitants are very induſtrious, and carry on a conſiderable Trade in Silk, Wine, and other Commodities. Near <hi>Catania</hi> are ſome Remains of an ancient Amphitheater, and a Naumachia, which ſhew the former Grandeur of this City.</p>
                  <p>I will now proceed to give you an account of the Iſland of <hi>Malta.</hi> We arrived at <hi>Valetta,</hi> the Capital City, on the 2d of <hi>December,</hi> after a Voyage of five Days from <hi>Meſſina.</hi> This City is ſo called from <hi>John de Valetta,</hi> Grand Maſter of the Order of St. <hi>John</hi> of <hi>Jeruſalem,</hi> who laid the firſt Stone of it in the Year 1566, after the famous Siege of <hi>Malta</hi> by the <hi>Turks.</hi> It is ſituated on a narrow Neck of Land between two large and ſecure Harbours, called the Great Port and <hi>Marſa Muſceit.</hi> The City is built upon a Rock, and is fortified with ſuch ſtrong and regular Works, that it is juſtly eſteemed one of the beſt fortified Places in the World. The Streets are ſtraight, and cut each other at right Angles, and are paved with white Stone. The Houſes are generally three Stories high, and built of the ſame Stone, which gives the City a very neat and uniform Appearance. The principal publick Buildings are the Grand Maſter's Palace, the Church of St. <hi>John,</hi> the Inns or Auberges of the ſeveral Languages, the Hoſpital, the Library, and the Arſenal.</p>
                  <p>The Grand Maſter's Palace is a magnificent Building, ſituated in the Center of the City. It contains ſeveral ſpacious Apartments, richly furniſhed, and adorned with fine Paintings and Tapeſtries. The Council-Chamber is particularly remarkable for its curious Ceiling, painted by <hi>Matteo Preti,</hi> called <hi>Il Calabrese,</hi> repreſenting the principal Events of the Order of St. <hi>John.</hi> In this Palace is alſo a fine Armoury, containing Arms for many thouſand Men, and ſeveral Trophies taken from the <hi>Turks.</hi>
                  </p>
                  <p>The Church of St. <hi>John</hi> is the Conventual Church of the Order, and is a very noble Edifice. The Pavement is entirely compoſed of rich Marble Slabs, under which are interred the Knights of the Order, each Slab being curiouſly inlaid with the Arms and Inſcriptions of the deceaſed Knight. The Roof is vaulted, and beautifully painted by <hi>Matteo Preti,</hi> with Scenes from the Life of St. <hi>John Baptiſt.</hi> There are ſeveral ſide Chapels belonging to the different Languages of the Order, each adorned with rich Altars, Paintings, and Monuments. The moſt remarkable is the Chapel of <hi>Arragon,</hi> where there is a famous Picture of the Beheading of St. <hi>John</hi> by <hi>Caravaggio.</hi>
                  </p>
                  <p>The Inns or Auberges of the eight Languages (<hi>Provence, Auvergne, France, Italy, Arragon, England, Germany,</hi> and <hi>Caſtile</hi>) are large and handſome Buildings, where the Knights of each Nation reſide. The Inn of <hi>Caſtile</hi> is particularly magnificent, with a very fine Façade.</p>
                  <p>The Hoſpital of the Knights of St. <hi>John</hi> is one of the moſt famous in <hi>Europe.</hi> It is a very large Building, capable of receiving many hundreds of Patients. The Sick are attended with the greateſt Care and Tenderneſs, and the Knights themſelves often ſerve them. The Utenſils used for the Sick, even for the pooreſt, are ſaid to be of Silver, to ensure Cleanlineſs and prevent Infection.</p>
                  <p>The Library contains a good Collection of Books, particularly in Hiſtory and Theology, and is open to the Publick at certain Hours.</p>
                  <p>The Fortifications of <hi>Valetta</hi> are truly admirable, conſiſting of a ſeries of Baſtions, Cavaliers, Ravelins, and Ditches, all cut out of the ſolid Rock. The principal Forts are St. <hi>Elmo,</hi> which defends the Entrance of both Harbours, and Fort <hi>Ricaſoli</hi> and Fort <hi>Tigné,</hi> which guard the <hi>Marſa Muſceit</hi> and the Great Harbour reſpectively. The Walls are of an incredible thickneſs, and mounted with a great number of Cannon.</p>
                  <p>The Iſland of <hi>Malta</hi> it ſelf is about 20 Miles long and 12 Miles broad. It is generally rocky and has little natural Soil, much of it having been brought from <hi>Sicily.</hi> However, by diligent cultivation, it produces Cotton, Fruits (eſpecially Figs and Oranges), and Honey of excellent Quality. It is not ſelf-ſufficient in Corn or Wine, which are imported, chiefly from <hi>Sicily.</hi> The ancient Capital, <hi>Citta Vecchia</hi> (or <hi>Mdina</hi>), is ſituated on a Hill in the interior of the Iſland and is a place of great antiquity, with Catacombs and old Palaces.</p>
                  <p>The Knights of <hi>Malta,</hi> originally the Knights Hoſpitallers of St. <p>John of Jeruſalem, have a long and glorious Hiſtory of defending Chriſtendom. After being driven from <hi>Jeruſalem</hi> and then <hi>Rhodes,</hi> they were granted <hi>Malta</hi> by Emperor <hi>Charles V</hi> in 1530. Their moſt famous exploit was the Great Siege of 1565, where they, under Grand Maſter <hi>Jean de Valette,</hi> heroically repelled a massive <hi>Ottoman</hi> invasion. The Order is composed of Knights from noble families of various European nations, divided into "Langues" or Tongues. Their primary vow, beſides poverty and chaſtity (often interpreted loosely), is the defence of the Faith and ſervice to the ſick.</p>
                  <p>The Government is headed by the Grand Maſter, who is elected for life by the Knights and holds ſovereign power over the Iſland. The Order maintains a ſmall but effective naval force, primarily to combat Barbary pirates.</p>
                  <p>The <hi>Malteſe</hi> people are devoutly Catholic, induſtrious, and ſpeak a language which is a dialect of Arabic with Italian and other Romance influences. They are generally welcoming to foreigners. The Iſland of <hi>Gozo,</hi> a ſmaller iſland nearby, is alſo under the rule of the Knights and is more fertile than <hi>Malta.</hi>
                  </p>
                  <p>I have found my time in <hi>Malta</hi> to be exceedingly intereſting, particularly in observing the unique blend of military prowess and religious devotion that characterizes the Order of St. <hi>John.</hi> The fortifications are truly a marvel of engineering, and the hiſtory of the Knights is palpable in every corner of <hi>Valetta.</hi>
                  </p>
